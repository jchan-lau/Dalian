Consensus Backbones for Directed Networks

A directed, redundancy-preserving sparsification (Chan-Lau 2026)

The consensus backbone (Chan-Lau 2026) is a directed dimensionality-reduction layer for dense weighted networks. Unlike the MST it preserves direction and redundancy: it is the union of several maximum directed spanning trees, capturing the dominant and the alternative spillover routes.

1. Why a directed backbone

Many economic linkages — risk spillovers, credit exposures, trade — are inherently directional. Collapsing them to an undirected MST discards the transmitter-receiver asymmetry that is often the whole point. The directed analogue of a spanning tree is an arborescence.

2. Normalisation: removing scale effects

A node connected by a few heavy edges can dominate one connected by many light edges purely because of scale. Normalising the adjacency matrix removes this bias, and the choice selects which facet to emphasise:

NormalisationDefinitionEmphasis
Row-stochasticEmission profiles — how each source distributes outgoing influence.
Column-stochasticReceiver vulnerability — each target’s dependence on its sources.
Doubly stochastic (Sinkhorn-Knopp)Scale-invariant latent structure. The recommended default.
The doubly-stochastic (Sinkhorn) normalisation iteratively rescales rows and columns until both sum to one, isolating exceptionally strong linkages net of node-specific scale. The tool checks for positive row/column support and verifies convergence to a numerical tolerance; it reports an error rather than labelling an infeasible scaling as doubly stochastic.

3. Maximum arborescences (Chu-Liu/Edmonds)

An arborescence rooted at node is a directed spanning tree in which has no incoming edge and every other node has exactly one, so a unique directed path runs from to each node. The maximum arborescence solves

The Chu-Liu/Edmonds algorithm solves it exactly: (i) each non-root node provisionally takes its strongest incoming edge; (ii) any cycle is contracted to a super-node with adjusted weights and the procedure recurses; (iii) the contraction is expanded to recover a valid tree. It is the directed counterpart of Prim/Kruskal.

4. Branching packs and the consensus union

A single arborescence can be too sparse. A branching pack computes diversified arborescences from the same root: after each, the edges it used are penalised by a factor , with and the number of prior uses, steering later trees toward fresh routes. The construction repeats across the top- roots by out-strength (the dominant emitters). The consensus backbone is the union of all these arborescences; each edge carries a frequency score — the number of arborescences selecting it.
