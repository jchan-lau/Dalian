The connectedness tables, networks, spanning trees and backbones produced by the tool all rest on one statistical object: a vector autoregression (VAR) and the forecast-error variance decomposition (FEVD) derived from it. This page explains that engine at the level of an intermediate econometrics course.

Stationarity and choosing the lag length

The VAR is stable when all eigenvalues of its companion matrix lie inside the unit circle; in practice this means working with returns or first differences rather than levels. The corrected tool estimates the largest companion-root modulus, reports it in the summary, warns when it is close to one, and blocks FEVD output when the estimate is at least one. The lag length trades bias against variance: too few lags leave dynamics in the residuals, too many waste degrees of freedom. Information criteria (AIC, BIC) give guidance, but in connectedness work is often fixed by convention. The tool lets you set directly.

3. The identification problem and two decompositions

3a. Orthogonalized (Cholesky) FEVD — order-dependent

Factor the covariance as with lower-triangular (the Cholesky factor). This imposes a recursive ordering: the first variable may shock all others contemporaneously, the second all but the first, and so on. The decomposition
