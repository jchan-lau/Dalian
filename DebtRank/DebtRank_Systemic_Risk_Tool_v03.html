DebtRank · Systemic Risk in Financial Networks

Based on the DebtRank framework of Battiston et al. (2012), Bardoscia et al. (2015), and Battiston et al. (2016)

Tool Method description Users’ guide

1 · Data

Provide an interbank financial network. Two CSV layouts are auto-detected:

  • Bilateral-exposure matrix — first column = bank name; then one column per bank giving the exposure Aij of lender i (row) to borrower j (column); plus two columns external_assets and equity.
  • Aggregate balance sheets — columns bank, interbank_lending, interbank_borrowing, external_assets, equity. The tool builds one deterministic sparse directed-fitness topology and uses RAS (iterative proportional fitting) to match the aggregate margins. This is a reproducible approximation inspired by Bardoscia et al. (2015), not their stochastic network ensemble.

2 · Stress scenario & model settings

Simplified third-round fire-sale scenario

The optional fire-sale calculation is a transparent heuristic extension inspired by the third-round channel in Battiston et al. (2016). It assumes asset sales proportional to each surviving bank’s equity loss; it does not solve the paper’s target-leverage restoration equation.

The linear dynamics lets every bank keep passing on distress as long as it keeps receiving it; the original dynamics lets each bank propagate only the first time it is hit. The original value is a lower bound for the linear one (Bardoscia et al. 2015).

3 · System-level systemic risk

Distress propagation over rounds

Global vulnerability H(t) = equity-weighted average loss. The shock is injected at round 1; rounds 2+ are the network amplification.

Loss decomposition by round

First round = direct shock on external assets. Second round = interbank distress propagation. Third round = simplified heuristic fire-sale losses (if enabled).

4 · Firm-level systemic risk

Two complementary scenario measures, using the Battiston et al. (2016)/original-DebtRank convention: impact = relative system-wide equity loss its own default would induce (its DebtRank); vulnerability = relative equity loss it suffers under the chosen scenario. These are not the single-bank external-asset-shock impact and cross-experiment average vulnerability used in Bardoscia et al. (2015, Fig. 2).

5 · Network & DebtRank diagram

Node size ∝ total assets; colour ∝ DebtRank impact; edge width ∝ exposure size. Choose how many exposures to draw, and the layout that best reveals the structure.

6 · Loss distribution — VaR & CVaR

Monte-Carlo over random systemic shocks (shock size drawn from a Beta distribution), comparing first-round-only losses with full network propagation. Network effects shift the loss distribution — and the Value-at-Risk — sharply to the right (Battiston et al. 2016).
